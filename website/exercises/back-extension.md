# 45-degree back extension

A hinge on a 45-degree bench with your hips on the pad and feet anchored. You lower your upper body, then rise until your body is one straight line.

Also called: hyperextension, roman chair back extension

- **Type:** Strength

- **Equipment:** Gym

- **Area:** Lower back

- **Level:** Advanced

- **Starting dose in Remedy:** 3 sets of 10

## How to do it

- Set the pad so it sits just below your hip bones and lock your heels under the foot pads.
- Start with your body in a straight line and your arms crossed on your chest.
- Hinge at the hips to lower your upper body toward the floor with a long back.
- Rise by squeezing your glutes until your body is one straight line again.

## Form cues

- Hinge down, then rise until the body is one line.
- Stop at straight; don’t arch past it.

## Common mistakes

- Arching back past a straight line at the top.
- Pad set too high, so you bend at the waist instead of the hips.

## Easier and harder versions

- **Easier:** [Hip hinge](https://remedyrecoveries.com/exercises/bodyweight-hip-hinge)

## Why it helps a sore back

It strengthens the glutes, hamstrings, and muscles along the spine together. Remedy places it late in a program, after the bodyweight hinge feels solid.
