# Single-leg glute bridge

A glute bridge on one leg, with the other leg held straight. It doubles the load on the working side and adds a balance challenge for the hips.

Also called: one-leg bridge, single leg hip bridge

- **Type:** Glutes and hips

- **Equipment:** No equipment

- **Area:** Lower back

- **Level:** Intermediate

- **Starting dose in Remedy:** 3 sets of 10 per side

## How to do it

- Lie on your back with your knees bent and feet flat.
- Straighten one leg so it lines up with the other thigh.
- Drive through the heel of the planted foot to lift your hips until your body forms a straight line.
- Keep both hips level at the top, then lower slowly.
- Finish your reps and switch legs.

## Form cues

- Drive through the planted heel.
- Keep both hips level at the top; don’t let one drop.

## Common mistakes

- The hip on the lifted-leg side dropping.
- Arching the low back to get height.

## Why it helps a sore back

In Remedy it is the step up from the two-leg bridge: instead of piling on more reps, the program moves you to a harder version.

## How Remedy uses it

In the app it is called **Single-Leg Glute Bridge**. Remedy is more likely to pick it if you say your back is bothered by standing or walking or working out. A typical starting dose is 3 sets of 10 per side, and the program adjusts reps as the weeks go on. Every exercise in the app has a demonstration video.

General fitness information, not medical advice. Stop any exercise that causes sharp pain, or pain, numbness, or tingling spreading down a leg.
