Back exercises, step by step

Every exercise in the Remedy app, with how to do it, form cues, common mistakes, and where it fits in a back program. General fitness information, not medical advice.

Move the spine and hips through a comfortable range. Remedy uses these to warm up.

Mobility

Cat-cow stretch

An all-fours movement that alternates rounding and arching your spine.

Open-book rotation

A side-lying rotation for the upper and middle back.

No equipment · Gentle Mobility

Chair thoracic extension

A gentle backward bend of the upper back over the top of a chair.

A lunge-position stretch for the front of the hip.

McKenzie press-up

A press-up from lying on your stomach where the hips stay on the floor and the lower back extends.

No equipment · Gentle

Core and stability

Hold the trunk steady while arms and legs move. The base everything else sits on.

Core and stability

Dead bug exercise

A core exercise done on your back: you slowly lower an opposite arm and leg while keeping your trunk still.

No equipment · Beginner Core and stability

Bird dog exercise

From your hands and knees, you reach one arm forward and the opposite leg back while keeping your back still.

No equipment · Beginner Core and stability

Side plank from the knees

A shorter-lever side plank with your knees on the floor.

No equipment · Intermediate Core and stability

Side plank

Glutes and hips

Hip extension strength, so the hips do the lifting instead of the low back.

Glutes and hips

Glute bridge

Lying on your back with knees bent, you drive through your heels to lift your hips.

No equipment · Beginner Glutes and hips

Single-leg glute bridge

A glute bridge on one leg, with the other leg held straight.

No equipment · Intermediate Glutes and hips

Barbell hip thrust

A loaded bridge with your upper back on a bench and a barbell across your hips.

Strength

Dumbbell Romanian deadlift

Kettlebell deadlift

How to use this library

Pick a few exercises from different groups rather than many from one. A useful routine covers mobility, core stability, and strength. Exercises for lower back pain has a ready-made starter routine, and core exercises for back pain shows how the stability exercises progress.

If you would rather not assemble it yourself, Remedy picks from this library for you based on your answers, sets the reps, and moves you to harder versions as you go.
