# Hip hinge

Bending forward by pushing your hips back while your spine stays long. It is the movement pattern behind deadlifts and behind picking things up safely.

Also called: hip hinge exercise, how to hip hinge

- **Type:** Strength

- **Equipment:** No equipment

- **Area:** Lower back

- **Level:** Beginner

- **Starting dose in Remedy:** 3 sets of 12

## How to do it

- Stand with your feet hip-width apart and your knees softly bent.
- Put your hands on the front of your hips.
- Push your hips straight back, letting your chest come forward while your back stays long.
- Go until you feel your hamstrings stretch, then stand up by driving your hips forward.

## Form cues

- Push the hips back, not the knees forward.
- Keep a long back and soft knees.

## Common mistakes

- Bending from the waist and rounding the back.
- Turning it into a squat by bending the knees too much.

## Easier and harder versions

- **To make it easier:** Hold a broomstick along your back, touching your head, upper back, and tailbone. If any point loses contact, you are rounding.

- **Harder:** [Banded Romanian deadlift](https://remedyrecoveries.com/exercises/banded-romanian-deadlift) or [Single-leg Romanian deadlift](https://remedyrecoveries.com/exercises/single-leg-romanian-deadlift)

## Why it helps a sore back

Learning to bend from the hips is one of the most useful things a person with a sore back can practice, because it is how you pick things up every day. Remedy uses it to build the pattern before adding load.

## How Remedy uses it

In the app it is called **Bodyweight Hip Hinge**. Remedy is more likely to pick it if you say your back is bothered by bending or lifting. A typical starting dose is 3 sets of 12, and the program adjusts reps as the weeks go on. Every exercise in the app has a demonstration video.

General fitness information, not medical advice. Stop any exercise that causes sharp pain, or pain, numbness, or tingling spreading down a leg.
