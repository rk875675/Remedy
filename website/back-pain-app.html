What to look for in a back pain app

Most “apps for back pain” are either a stretch library or a clinical product you get through work. The first has no plan. The second is not something you buy on the App Store.

A useful consumer back pain app should do four things:

  • Ask about your back, history, and activity before assigning work.
  • Give you one multi-week program, not a searchable pile of clips.
  • Show every exercise on video so you are not guessing form.
  • Make it obvious what to do today, in a few minutes.

That is the product Remedy is. v1 is back only — not neck, shoulder, hip, or knee.

How to work on back pain with an app

Use the app as the plan, not as a search engine. Open it, do the session it queued, check in on how you feel, close it. Repeating a short session beats collecting new videos.

Do not use any app — including Remedy — as care. If pain is severe or acute, or you have numbness, weakness, loss of bladder or bowel control, pain after a fall or injury, fever, or unexplained weight loss, see a healthcare provider. Remedy is for people 16 and older who have already been cleared to do general exercise.
