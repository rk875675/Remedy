Back pain apps compared: which one fits your situation

The right back pain app depends less on features and more on your situation: whether your employer covers one, whether you want a care team, and what you are willing to pay.

Employer-covered digital PT: Hinge Health and Sword

Hinge Health and Sword Health (now branded simply Sword) are the two largest digital musculoskeletal programs in the US. Both pair app-based exercise therapy with clinical support, and both are usually offered as a benefit through employers and health plans rather than sold directly to individuals. Sword acquired Kaia Health in January 2026.

If your job or plan offers either at no cost, it is worth checking first. You get a care team that Remedy does not have. If you don’t have access, they generally aren’t something you can just download and buy.

Pain education apps: Curable

Curable takes a different approach: pain science education, meditation, and other mind-body techniques for chronic pain. It is a self-guided subscription app. It is less about exercise and more about how the nervous system processes pain, so it can sit alongside an exercise program rather than replace one.

Free videos

There are good back exercise videos online for free. What they don’t give you is a plan: what to do today, what to do next week, and when to make it harder. Most people who start with a playlist stop within a couple of weeks, which matters because exercise for back pain works over weeks, not days 1.

In-person physical therapy

A physical therapist can examine you, which no app can. If you have warning signs, aren’t improving after a few weeks, or simply want a professional assessment, in-person care is the right call.

Remedy

Remedy is a consumer iPhone app. You answer a short onboarding about your back, get a multi-week program built from your answers, and follow short sessions with a demonstration video for every exercise. You check in on your pain before and after each session so you can see the trend. It is free to download; full access is a subscription of $4.99 a week, $12.99 a month, or $79.99 a year, with a free trial where offered. Pricing details.

What it isn’t: physical therapy, a diagnosis, or a care team. There are no live coaches, no motion sensors, and no Android app yet.

How to choose

  • Warning signs, or a new injury? See a clinician first.
  • Covered by work or insurance? Try Hinge Health or Sword.
  • Chronic pain where stress and worry play a big part? Curable may be a useful addition.
  • Want a structured exercise plan you can start today on iPhone? The Remedy app.

Whatever you choose, exercise is one of the best-supported non-drug options for chronic low back pain 23. The best program is the one you will actually do for six weeks.

Questions

What is the best app for back pain?

It depends on access. If your employer or health plan covers a digital physical therapy program, that is usually the best first choice. If not, a consumer exercise app with a structured, progressive program is the closest self-pay option.

Is there a back pain app I can use without insurance or an employer?

Yes. Remedy is available to anyone with an iPhone on the App Store. It is free to download, and full access is a subscription.

Do back pain apps actually work?

Exercise is one of the best-supported options for chronic low back pain, and apps are one way to deliver it. Results depend mostly on whether you keep doing the program for several weeks.

When to see someone instead of using an app

Get medical care, not an exercise routine, if you have back pain with any of these:

  • Numbness or tingling around your genitals or buttocks
  • Trouble peeing, or loss of bladder or bowel control
  • Numbness, tingling, or weakness in one or both legs that is getting worse
  • Back pain after a fall, car accident, or other injury
  • A fever, or feeling unwell, along with back pain
  • Unexplained weight loss, or a history of cancer
  • Pain that is severe, constant, and gets worse at night or when lying down
  • Pain that keeps getting worse over several weeks instead of better
