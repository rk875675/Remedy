Back exercises with no equipment

You do not need a gym to look after a sore back. You need a floor, a few minutes, and a routine that gets slightly harder over time.

By the Remedy team · Published October 15, 2026 · Updated October 23, 2026 · 2 min read · 2 sources

Key takeaways

  • A useful home routine covers mobility, core stability, and hip and back strength.
  • Progress by moving to harder versions, not by adding endless reps.
  • Sharp pain or pain spreading down a leg means stop that movement.

What does a home back routine need?

A useful back routine is not a random set of stretches. It covers three things:

  • Mobility: moving your spine and hips through a comfortable range. Examples: cat-cow stretch, open-book rotation, and knees rocking side to side while lying on your back.
  • Core stability: holding your trunk steady while your arms and legs move. Examples: bird dog, dead bug, and side plank from the knees.
  • Hip and back strength: the muscles that do the work when you lift, carry, and climb stairs. Examples: glute bridge, single-leg glute bridge, bodyweight squat, and split squat.

Guidelines like NICE’s recommend exercise programs for low back pain without naming one “best” exercise 1, and research comparing exercise types finds benefits across several 2. What matters more is doing a sensible mix regularly.

Progress by getting harder, not just longer

Doing thirty easy glute bridges is less useful than moving on to a harder version. A simple ladder looks like this: glute bridge, then single-leg bridge. For hinges, the hip hinge leads to the single-leg Romanian deadlift. For stability work, short holds of around eight to ten seconds, repeated, tend to work better than one long shaky hold.

Remedy works the same way: once an exercise reaches 15 reps, the program swaps in a harder variant instead of adding more. This is also why a routine needs weeks to show results; how long to stick with it covers what to expect.

Keeping it safe

  • Move slowly and with control. Speed is not the goal.
  • Mild, general discomfort that settles afterwards is common when you start. Sharp pain, or pain that spreads down a leg, is a reason to stop that movement.
  • If an exercise always makes things worse, swap it for another in the same group.

Mornings are a good time for the mobility part if you wake up stiff. If your back trouble started in the gym, the gym guide covers how to train around it.

Questions

Can you strengthen your back without equipment?

Yes. Bodyweight exercises like the bird dog, glute bridge, single-leg bridge, side plank, and hip hinge build real strength when you progress them to harder versions over time.

How many times a week should I do a home back routine?

Three or more short sessions a week is a common target. Consistency over several weeks matters more than the length of any one session.

When to see someone instead of using an app

Get medical care, not an exercise routine, if you have back pain with any of these:

  • Numbness or tingling around your genitals or buttocks
  • Trouble peeing, or loss of bladder or bowel control
  • Numbness, tingling, or weakness in one or both legs that is getting worse
  • Back pain after a fall, car accident, or other injury
  • A fever, or feeling unwell, along with back pain
  • Unexplained weight loss, or a history of cancer
  • Pain that is severe, constant, and gets worse at night or when lying down
  • Pain that keeps getting worse over several weeks instead of better
